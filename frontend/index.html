WildCast

Daily probability of a wildlife encounter — a weather forecast for the wild.

How WildCast works

Curated areas are 10 hand-vetted places (Yellowstone, Kruger, Brisbane, Svalbard, the Amazon, the Serengeti, Borneo, Patagonia, the Scottish Highlands, and Costa Rica's cloud forest) with real sighting records and real Open-Meteo weather trained into a calibrated model ahead of time — predictions load instantly.

Explore anywhere lets you click any point on Earth. WildCast checks, live, whether each of its known species has ever actually been recorded — by GBIF, iNaturalist, or (for birds) eBird — near that exact spot, then scores the ones that pass using real live weather for that location and the trained model of whichever curated area is geographically closest. Checking multiple independent sources, instead of just one, means a species confirmed by more than one database gets a modest confidence boost. It's slower (a few seconds, since it's checking real data on the fly) and only covers WildCast's existing 50 species — but the location itself can be anywhere.

Confidence reflects how many real historical sighting records back a prediction: high 150+, medium 40–149, low under 40. Low-confidence predictions are still real, just backed by less data.

Population & ability to evade: when available, predictions also note a species' real IUCN Red List conservation status (rarer/declining species are naturally harder to encounter) and its natural activity pattern (a nocturnal or crepuscular species is genuinely harder to see than a day-active one) — both shown as plain-language context, never as invented statistics.

The banner at the top of the page always says whether the predictions you're looking at come from real ingested data or the offline demo dataset — see the data source note below.

Is this real data? The banner at the top of the page always states it plainly. WildCast's backend trains on real observation records from GBIF, iNaturalist, and (for birds) eBird, plus real Open-Meteo weather and (when configured) real IUCN Red List conservation status — but it can also run on an offline synthetic demo dataset (clearly labeled as such) when no real ingestion has been run yet, so the app is still explorable without live API access.
